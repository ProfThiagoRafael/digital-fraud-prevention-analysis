## 1. Project Overview

This repository documents a **real-world cybersecurity investigation** conducted following the receipt of a suspicious SMS message targeting an individual with threats involving bank account suspension and restrictions related to Brazilian taxpayer identification and business registration.

Unlike a simulated laboratory exercise, this investigation originated from an actual incident involving a real individual who requested technical assistance to determine whether the communication was legitimate or fraudulent.

The investigation involved examining the message, identifying social engineering indicators, researching the telephone contact and administrative protocol presented, comparing the communication with publicly documented fraud patterns, and providing preventive security guidance.

The primary objective was to identify potential fraud before the recipient interacted with the suspicious contact.

**This case demonstrates practical cybersecurity analysis applied to a real situation, with an emphasis on prevention, evidence preservation, risk assessment, and digital security awareness.**

## 3. Incident Description

The affected individual received an SMS message claiming that bank accounts would be suspended and that restrictions would be imposed on taxpayer identification and business registration.

The message also included:
- An administrative-looking protocol number.
- A specific deadline for taking action.
- A telephone number presented as a resolution channel.
- Language designed to create urgency and concern.

The communication attempted to direct the recipient toward a telephone contact rather than an independently verified institutional channel.

These characteristics raised concerns about a possible social engineering operation involving a fake support or financial regularization service.

### 3.1. Anonymized Incident Representation

The original communication contained personal and operational identifiers.

For privacy and security reasons, this repository does not reproduce the original message or its identifying details. All identifying information has been replaced with fictional data.

The original evidence remains restricted to the investigation documentation.

## 4. Investigation Objectives

The investigation was designed to:

1. Identify potential indicators of digital fraud.
2. Examine the psychological manipulation techniques used in the message.
3. Analyze the credibility of the displayed sender and administrative protocol.
4. Investigate the telephone contact through available public information.
5. Compare the communication with known fraud patterns.
6. Consult official institutional security guidance.
7. Assess the recipient's potential exposure.
8. Establish preventive recommendations.
9. Produce formal technical documentation.

## 5. Investigation Methodology

The investigation followed a structured technical process.

### 5.1. Evidence Collection and Preservation

The initial analysis included:
- Preservation of the original SMS screenshot.
- Extraction of the message content.
- Identification of the displayed sender.
- Recording of the telephone number.
- Recording of the administrative protocol.
- Documentation of the deadline imposed by the message.

Original evidence was retained separately and was not included in the public repository.

### 5.2. Social Engineering Analysis

The message was examined for common psychological manipulation techniques.

| Indicator | Technical Interpretation |
|---|---|
| Financial intimidation | Threat of bank account suspension |
| Administrative intimidation | Alleged taxpayer registration restrictions |
| Artificial urgency | Short deadline for contacting the sender |
| Authority simulation | Administrative-looking protocol |
| Telephone redirection | Instruction to contact an unverified number |
| Psychological pressure | Fear of financial and administrative consequences |

The combination of these indicators is consistent with patterns commonly associated with social engineering.

### 5.3. External Research and OSINT

Publicly available information was examined to evaluate the credibility of the communication.

The research considered:
- Public complaints involving similar SMS messages.
- Information concerning short-code messaging.
- Public references associated with the telephone number.
- Similar administrative protocol patterns.
- Official warnings about financial and tax-related scams.

The investigation did not establish the identity of the actual sender or the operator responsible for the specific message.

### 5.4. Institutional Validation

The findings were compared against public security guidance issued by:
- Brazilian Federal Revenue Service.
- Brazilian Federation of Banks (FEBRABAN).
- Brazilian Ministry of Communications.
- CERT.br.

This comparison helped distinguish legitimate administrative procedures from suspicious communication patterns.

### 6.1. Threat Classification

**Classification: High suspicion of digital fraud involving social engineering.**

The classification is based on the combined behavioral indicators and similarities to documented fraudulent approaches.

It does not constitute proof of criminal authorship or establish that a specific individual or company committed a crime.

## 7. Victim Exposure Assessment

A preventive interview was conducted to determine whether the recipient had interacted with the suspicious communication.

The assessment included:
- Whether the telephone number had been contacted.
- Whether personal information had been disclosed.
- Whether banking credentials had been shared.
- Whether authentication codes had been provided.
- Whether any payment or financial transaction had occurred.

### 7.1. Assessment Results

| Verification | Result |
|---|---|
| Contact with the suspicious telephone number | No |
| Personal information disclosed | No |
| Banking credentials disclosed | No |
| Authentication codes disclosed | No |
| Financial transaction reported | No |

The recipient did not contact the telephone number or disclose information.

**The preventive intervention occurred before any reported interaction with the suspicious contact.**

No evidence of credential exposure or financial loss resulting from this particular interaction was identified during the assessment.

## 8. Preventive Security Measures

The following recommendations were provided to the affected individual:

1. Do not call telephone numbers supplied in suspicious messages.
2. Never disclose banking passwords or authentication codes.
3. Verify taxpayer registration through official government portals.
4. Check business registration and tax obligations through authorized services.
5. Contact financial institutions through previously verified channels.
6. Preserve suspicious digital communications as evidence.
7. Report suspicious messages to the relevant telecommunications provider.
8. Contact the bank immediately if financial credentials are subsequently exposed.
